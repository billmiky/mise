ingredients:
- 4 teaspoons vegetable oil, divided
- 5 ounces (5 cups) baby spinach
- 1 carrot, peeled and shredded
- 1 scallion, minced
- 1 tablespoon soy sauce
- 1 garlic clove, minced
- 1 teaspoon sugar
- 2 cups cooked white rice (this page)
- 1 recipe Fried Eggs (this page)
- ¾ cup Sautéed Mushrooms with Sesame and Ginger (this page), warmed
- ½ cup kimchi, chopped coarse
- 2 tablespoons Gochujang Sauce (this page)
- 'For the gochujang sauce (makes ½ cup):'
- ¼ cup gochujang
- 3 tablespoons water
- 2 tablespoons toasted sesame oil
- 1 teaspoon sugar
directions:
- Heat 1 teaspoon oil in 12-inch nonstick skillet over medium-high heat until shimmering. Add spinach, carrot, scallion, soy
  sauce, garlic, and sugar and cook, stirring frequently, until spinach is wilted and carrots are warmed through, 1 to 2 minutes.
  Transfer vegetables to bowl, cover with aluminum foil to keep warm, and set aside until ready to serve.
- Heat remaining 1 tablespoon oil in now-empty skillet over medium-high heat until shimmering. Add rice and firmly press into
  compact, even layer, cover, and cook, without stirring, until rice begins to crisp, about 2 minutes. Uncover, reduce heat
  to medium, and continue to cook until bottom of rice is golden brown, 4 to 6 minutes. Season with salt and pepper to taste.
  Divide among individual serving bowls then top with spinach mixture, fried eggs, mushrooms, and kimchi. Drizzle with gochujang
  sauce. Serve.
- 'Gochujang sauce: Whisk all ingredients together in bowl. (Sauce can be refrigerated for up to 3 days.)'

Gochujang sauce: Spicy Korean gochujang, which helps create this quick and flavorful sauce, is sold in Asian markets and many supermarkets. If you can’t find it, substitute sriracha and omit the water.

Why This Recipe Works: The combination of rice, vegetables, and egg in Korean bibimbap is a grouping made in bowl heaven, but we knew we needed to minimize the preparation time and maximize flavor to turn this dish into a weeknight-friendly bowl for two. Traditional bibimbap can have a lengthy list of ingredients; one of our favorite versions also requires a hot stone pot that gives the rice base a crisped, crunchy texture. To bring this bowl home (without the stone pot), we started by cutting the sautéed veggie list to just spinach, carrots, and mushrooms. While they stayed warm, we fried up our cooked rice in the same pan. We gave the rice a few minutes in the pan before serving, which allowed it to develop a golden brown crunchy bottom. To top our bowls, we fried two eggs, which gave each bowl a runny yolk that combined with our gochujang sauce into a spicy-rich sauce. Store-bought kimchi, with its bold fermented flavor, was the ideal final accompaniment.
