ingredients:
- 12 leaves of iceberg lettuce, torn into cups, or tender lettuce leaves, such as cos or baby gem
- 2 tbsp flavourless oil (such as sunflower or grapeseed) or coconut oil
- 300g firm tofu, patted dry and crumbled into small chunks
- 200g fresh corn kernels, removed from 3 cobs of corn, or drained canned sweetcorn, patted dry with kitchen paper
- 100g green beans, trimmed and cut into 5mm rounds
- 1 small red onion or 2 medium shallots (120g), peeled and thinly sliced
- 6 tbsp toasted glutinous rice powder
- 90ml freshly squeezed lime juice (about 4 limes), plus extra if needed
- 50ml soy sauce, plus extra if needed
- 2 tsp coconut sugar or brown sugar
- ½–1 tsp chilli powder, plus extra to serve
- fine sea salt, to taste
- 2 handfuls of mixed tender herbs (such as coriander, Thai basil, and/or mint), roughly chopped
- 2 spring onions, thinly sliced
directions:
- Soak the lettuce in a bowl of ice-cold water to crisp up while you prepare the other ingredients. Drain well once ready
  to use.
- Heat the oil in a large frying pan on a high heat. Add the tofu and cook for 2–3 minutes, stirring, until about half of
  the tofu looks golden and is beginning to become crispy. Add the corn kernels and cook for 1 minute on a high heat, stirring
  occasionally, until they take on some colour. Add the green beans for 30 seconds, then remove the tofu mixture from the
  pan, transfer to a large bowl and set aside.
- Add the onion or shallots to the tofu mixture, along with 4 tablespoons toasted glutinous rice powder, and stir together.
- Stir the lime, soy and sugar together in a small bowl. Season the sauce with the chilli powder to taste. Add the sauce to
  the tofu larb and combine together; season with salt, extra lime or soy as needed.
- To serve, stir in the chopped herbs and spring onions. Place a spoonful of the larb onto the lettuce leaves and top with
  extra rice powder, and a small pinch of chilli powder, if you can handle the heat.

In this vegan take on larb, the fragrant minced meat salad famed in Laos and Thailand, golden crumbles of crisp, pan-seared tofu combine with charred sweetcorn and green beans to absorb the piercing flavours of the tangy soy, lime and chilli dressing. Toasted glutinous rice powder adds a heavenly crunch, so be sure not to leave it out: it is an essential element of larb, and adds a distinctive nutty and toasty flavour. Crisp iceberg lettuce encases the punchy larb, which is laced with aromatic herbs. The tofu larb filling can be served piping hot, at room temperature or even cold. The dish bursts with freshness; it’s one that shouts rather than whispers, and tastes as beautiful as it looks.
