ingredients:
- 2 good-quality chicken schnitzels (about 250g) or breaded chicken fillets, or Homemade Chicken Katsu, or 2 good-quality
  pork schnitzels (about 250g), or 6–8 frozen fish fingers
- flavourless oil, such as sunflower or grapeseed
- 400g cooked white rice (optional)
- shop-bought tonkatsu sauce (or use ketchup, HP or barbecue sauce), to serve
- 100g white cabbage, very finely shredded
- 1 spring onion, sliced diagonally
- sesame seeds, preferably toasted
- 2 tbsp pickled ginger, drained (optional)
- 4 tsp Kewpie or ordinary mayonnaise
- 1 tsp Dijon mustard
- lemon wedges (for the pork schnitzel; optional)
- 2 sheets crispy seaweed, such as roasted seaweed sheets or squares of nori sheet (optional)
- 'Homemade Chicken Katsu:'
- 2 chicken breasts, skinless and boneless (about 320g)
- fine sea salt, to taste
- 2 tsp garam masala
- 2 tsp smoked paprika
- 150g plain flour
- 2 eggs, beaten
- 150g panko breadcrumbs
- flavourless oil (such as sunflower or grapeseed), for deep-frying
directions:
- If using pork or chicken schnitzel, heat 4 tablespoons oil in a large non-stick frying pan on a medium heat. Once hot, add
  both schnitzels and cook for around 3–4 minutes, or until golden on the underside. Flip them over, reduce the heat to medium-low,
  and cook for 3–4 more minutes, or until golden. If using a thermometer, the pork or chicken is cooked when its internal
  temperature reaches at least 75°C. Transfer to a kitchen paper-lined tray to drain.
- If using Homemade Chicken Katsu, prepare as per the method. If using fish fingers, cook them according to the instructions
  on the packet.
- Divide the cooked white rice between two bowls. Drizzle each bowl of rice with 1 tablespoon tonkatsu sauce, then top with
  shredded cabbage. Drizzle another ½ tablespoon tonkatsu over the cabbage, then place the schnitzels or fish fingers on top.
- Drizzle over one more tablespoon of tonkatsu sauce and top with spring onions and a sprinkle of sesame seeds. Finally, add
  1 tablespoon pickled ginger, 2 teaspoons of kewpie mayonnaise and ½ teaspoon of mustard to each bowl. Serve with a lemon
  wedge and some crispy seaweed, if using.
- 'Homemade Chicken Katsu: Preheat the oven to 200°C/180°C fan. Slice through each chicken breast lengthways to make thinner
  fillets. Season with salt. Mix the spices in one bowl, the flour and ½ tablespoon salt in another, the eggs in another and
  the panko in a final bowl.'
- Heat 120ml oil in a medium, heavy-based saucepan to a temperature of 180°C. Dust the chicken pieces with the spices and
  then dip in the flour bowl, shaking off any excess before dipping them into the eggs, then the panko.
- Deep-fry the chicken 2 pieces at a time for 4–5 minutes each. Turn after 2 minutes to ensure they cook evenly. Remove with
  tongs and drain on a kitchen paper-lined baking tray to drain excess oil. Transfer to a baking tray and bake for 6 minutes,
  or until cooked through. Rest the chicken for 5 minutes, then slice into finger-length pieces.

Serving a hot bowl of katsudon to prime suspects during police interrogations to get a confession is a common trope in Japanese films and anime, such is the dish’s reputation as soul food that can melt even the coldest of criminal hearts.

Katsudon is an auspicious food too, and is often eaten by Japanese students the night before a major exam because katsu is a homophone for the Japanese word that means ‘to win’. Basically, this dish is so powerful that you’d be wise to cook it for loved ones for good luck, or when you want the cold, hard truth (like, who did eat the last brownie in the fridge?).

The dish is typically served with tonkatsu (deep-fried pork), but chicken katsu is also popular, and I’ve included a fish finger option because, let’s face it, they have universal appeal. You could swap in the Panko Sambal Aubergine for a vegan version. Serve with microwave rice for speed, or old-fashioned home-cooked rice if you have time.

Homemade Chicken Katsu (sub-recipe on page): Chicken katsu works brilliantly as the star for an endless number of dishes. Making it at home is surprisingly quick, and the flavour pay-off is succulent, tender fillets of chicken, crispy and golden in a salty panko crumb. I can’t stress enough how useful a digital thermometer is for deep-frying. You’ll know when the chicken is cooked through, and it’s the safest way to regulate the heat. Prep & cooking time 25 minutes. Serves 2.
