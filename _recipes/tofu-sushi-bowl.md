ingredients:
- 7 ounces firm tofu, cut into 3-inch-long by ½-inch-thick fingers
- ⅛ teaspoon table salt
- ⅛ teaspoon pepper
- 3 tablespoons cornstarch
- 1 tablespoon vegetable oil
- 2 cups cooked brown rice (this page)
- ¼ cup Sesame-Scallion Vinaigrette (this page)
- 3 radishes, sliced thin
- ½ cucumber, halved lengthwise, seeded, and sliced thin
- ½ ripe avocado, cubed
- 1 (8 by 7½-inch) sheet nori, crumbled
- 'For the sesame-scallion vinaigrette (makes ¾ cup):'
- ¼ cup soy sauce
- 2 tablespoons rice vinegar
- 2 tablespoons mirin
- 2 tablespoons water
- 1 teaspoon chili oil (optional)
- ½ teaspoon toasted sesame oil
- 1 scallion, minced
directions:
- Spread tofu over paper towel–lined plate, let drain for 20 minutes, then gently pat dry with paper towels. Sprinkle with
  salt and pepper.
- Gently toss drained tofu with cornstarch in bowl. Heat oil in 12-inch nonstick skillet over medium-high heat until shimmering.
  Add tofu and brown lightly on all sides, 12 to 15 minutes; transfer to clean paper towel–lined plate to drain.
- Toss rice with half of vinaigrette to coat then season with salt and pepper to taste. Divide among individual serving bowls
  then top with tofu, radishes, cucumber, and avocado. Drizzle with remaining vinaigrette and sprinkle with nori. Serve.
- 'Vinaigrette: Whisk all ingredients together in bowl. (Vinaigrette can be refrigerated for up to 3 days; whisk to recombine
  before using.)'

Sesame-scallion vinaigrette: This simple, flavorful vinaigrette, which comes together in just seconds, is light enough to add subtle flavor, as in our Tofu Sushi Bowl (this page). Just 1 teaspoon of chili oil packs in enough heat to elevate the potent combination of soy, mirin, sesame, and vinegar. For a milder sauce, omit the chili oil.

Why This Recipe Works: Sushi rolls can be both time-consuming and tedious to make, which is why they are often considered takeout fare. We wanted to develop a deconstructed version in a bowl that was easy to assemble at home with flavors inspired by sushi rolls. For simplicity, we kept it vegetarian with tofu, coating long fingers with cornstarch to help them brown to crispy perfection. Brown rice isn’t typical for sushi rolls, but we liked the added nutritional value and nutty chew in our bowl base. Sushi rice is often seasoned, so we tossed our rice with Sesame-Scallion Vinaigrette to stand in for the classic sushi rice seasoning. For toppings we achieved a fresh, satisfying crunch from some thinly sliced radishes and cucumber. Sushi can’t say no to avocado, and we loved its added richness. Some crumbled nori (to mimic the roll), scattered across the top of our sushi bowl sealed the deal. We like this bowl with either warm or room-temperature rice.
