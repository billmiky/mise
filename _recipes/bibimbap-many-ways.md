ingredients:
- 'Your choice of topping (halve the quantities):'
- Kecap Manis-Glazed Tofu, or
- Garlic and Ginger Prawns, or
- Sesame- and Soy-Lacquered Chicken, or Lucky Beef
- 'Sauce:'
- 5 tbsp gochujang paste
- 20ml rice wine vinegar or cider vinegar
- 10ml soy sauce
- 10ml sesame oil, plus extra for drizzling
- 1 tsp sesame oil, plus extra to serve
- 'The Rest:'
- 100g shiitake mushrooms, stems removed and thinly sliced
- 1 tsp soy sauce
- 80g baby spinach leaves
- 50g beansprouts
- 2 eggs
- flavourless oil, such as sunflower or grapeseed
- fine sea salt, to taste
- 400g cooked white rice
- ½ carrot, peeled and cut into thin matchsticks
- 3 radishes, thinly sliced (optional)
- 80g cucumber, halved lengthways, deseeded and sliced
- 3 tbsp good-quality kimchi
- crispy seaweed, such as roasted seaweed flakes or nori seaweed (optional)
- 2 tsp sesame seeds, preferably toasted
directions:
- Cook your chosen topping and set aside. Stir together the ingredients for the sauce in a bowl, and set aside. Place a large
  frying pan on a medium-high heat. Add the sesame oil, mushrooms and soy sauce. Cook, stirring often, for about 4 minutes,
  or until the mushrooms are cooked through. Set aside.
- Boil the kettle, then pour boiling water over the spinach leaves and beansprouts in a heatproof bowl. Leave for 3–5 minutes,
  then drain and set aside. Prepare the rest of the toppings.
- To fry the eggs, wipe out the pan. Add 1 tablespoon oil on a medium-high heat. Once the oil is shimmering, crack the eggs
  into the oil. Cook the eggs until the whites are cooked through but the yolks are still runny (or cooked to your liking).
  Season with a pinch of salt.
- To serve, divide the rice between the two bowls. Place the fried egg in the middle, then arrange each topping around the
  bowl. Drizzle each bowl with 1 teaspoon sesame oil.
- To serve, invite your guests to drizzle some sauce over their bowl. Mix together and enjoy.

The Korean dish of bibimbap translates as ‘mixing rice’: it’s a warm rice bowl topped with a curated selection of ingredients and seasonings such as gochujang and sesame oil. The dish, always a colourful feast for the eyes, comprises elements made with care and purpose, each chosen for their unique flavour profile, texture and balance.

Bibimbap is plated artfully, but should be mixed before serving. It’s often stirred with chopsticks but commonly eaten with a spoon because it can hold more ingredients, and therefore textures and flavours, at once, making each mouthful more enjoyable.

My version allows you to customise your bowl with your chosen topping. Apart from the fried egg and mushrooms, the rest is largely no-cook, and you can even use microwavable rice for ease. Koreans often make bibimbap in the home using leftover side dishes, so feel free to swap and change the toppings below to whatever takes your fancy.

Prep & cooking time 25–40 minutes, depending on your topping

Serves 2
